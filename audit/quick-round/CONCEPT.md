# Concept

**Quick round is the TikTok of cramming for the JLPT N2.**

The learner sits N2 on Sunday 6 December 2026. They work from 9 am to 8 or
10 pm on weekdays and are tired when they study. Studied "properly", they go
slowly over a few things. Quick round is built for the opposite: open it at
any spare moment, tap, tap, tap, and gain experience by going fast and seeing
the same things hundreds of times. The target is **1,000 questions a day**.

In the learner's words:

- "I don't think. You think. I just act and get experience from going fast
  and seeing the same things hundreds of times."
- "As simple and fast as it is to swipe on TikTok. Easy to boot and use
  anytime, even when I have only a few seconds to spare, all day long."
- "Mistakes are part of the game, just keep going."
- "Try to speak up when you read."
- "Reward speed and punish mistakes heavily."
- "This is not shogi, this is blitz chess."

## Principles

1. **The player never thinks about the app.** Every action is one tap; no
   dialogs, forms or mode choices inside the loop.
2. **Boots into a question.** No start screen.
3. **Speed.** Seconds per question; short transitions; nothing waits on a
   network (it works offline).
4. **Volume and repetition.** The same items come back many times; that is
   the point.
5. **Mistakes are part of the game.** Shown briefly, then on to the next.
6. **Say it out loud.** A reminder on every card, never enforced, no
   microphone.
7. **Honest scoring.** A blind guess never pays better than "I don't know".
   Never a pass probability.
8. **Nothing is lost.** Each answer is saved as it is given.
9. **The flag is one tap.** It saves the question as shown; the maintainer
   works out what was wrong later.

## Where it fits

It lives inside Echo, a local-first app for learning Japanese, as a view of
its N2 page (`/n2-mock.html?view=game`), so it shares Echo's N2 material and
the learner's history. It can be its own entry point: a Settings switch opens
Echo straight on it, and the home screen has a shortcut.

Its misses feed Echo's other N2 tools: Evening recall before bed, the Night
Radio (quiet replay during sleep of what was studied that day) and the next
morning's checks.
