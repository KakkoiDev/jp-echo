# What makes a question N1 (read with the author or editor brief)

The first N1 drafts had N1 prose and N3 questions. Hold every N1 item to this.

- **Reading asks what is not said outright.** Nearly every reading question is about the writer's stance, the point of an example, what an underlined phrase means in context, what follows from the argument, or how two views differ. A question whose answer is one stated fact ("why was he late?") does not belong, and neither does a wrong choice the text explicitly denies. Each wrong choice is something the text says or nearly says, attached to the wrong claim: a concession taken for the conclusion, the view the writer argues against, an example taken for the thesis, a true detail that does not answer the question.
- **Passages argue.** They set up a common view, qualify or overturn it, and end on a claim the reader must infer in part. Abstract nouns, long modifying clauses, written connectives (とはいえ, もっとも, いわば, さりとて), no furigana-level simplification.
- **文法形式の判断**: all four choices are real N1 or N2 patterns that attach to the same form (であれ / であろうと / といえども / ならいざしらず), so only meaning and register decide. Never a non-form such as であるに.
- **文の組み立て**: five-to-seven-word fragments with nested modification, where two orders look plausible until the whole sentence is read.
- **文脈規定 and 言い換え**: near-synonyms distinguished by collocation or nuance (見解 / 見地 / 観点 / 視点).
- **用法**: each wrong sentence uses the word in a neighbouring but impossible sense or collocation, not in an unrelated one.
- **漢字読み**: readings that an advanced learner actually confuses (irregular readings, 連濁, 呉音 against 漢音), not random syllables.
- **Listening**: speakers imply, hedge and change their minds; the answer is the speaker's real position or the option that survives several objections. 即時応答 turns on keigo direction, set phrases and indirect refusal, with all three replies grammatical and polite.

## 即時応答 at N1 (the block that stays too easy longest)

A plain question with a plain answer (opening hours, the way to a bank, repeating what was said) is N4. At N1 the utterance means more than it says, and the right reply answers what was meant:

- a complaint or regret said sideways (もう少し早く言ってくれればよかったのに／さすがにこの量は一人では…／言ってくれれば手伝ったのに) → the reply apologises, explains, or takes the hint;
- a hint that is really a request or a refusal (あいにくその日はちょっと…／できればそうしていただけると助かるんですが／検討はしてみますが…) → the reply accepts the refusal or acts on the request;
- praise, modesty and set exchanges of working life (おかげさまで／とんでもないです／お手数をおかけします／恐れ入ります) → the reply is the conventional counterpart;
- idiom and N1 grammar in speech (〜にこしたことはない／〜ないことには／〜っこない／〜ようがない／手が回らない／骨が折れる／頭が上がらない) → the reply shows it was understood.

All three replies are natural, polite Japanese that someone could say in that room. The two wrong ones answer the literal words, take the speaker for pleased when they are annoyed (or the reverse), reply as the wrong person (accepting thanks that were not given, apologising for the other's fault), or answer a hint as if it were a question of fact. Utterances are 20–45 characters of real speech.
