# How Echo should earn the learner’s confidence

The December 6 target leaves 12 pre-exam study days from November 24 in Japan. Start with a baseline today. Skip the two earlier ramp-up days in a full 14-day plan. Use this as a coaching protocol alongside the live planner. Several decisions below still require the learner to follow the indicated route.

| Days before exam | Work | Evidence that determines tomorrow |
|---|---|---|
| 14–13 if available | Set the date and first-pass deadline. Learn the controls and answer a short sample from all four skills. | Separate missing knowledge, slow recall and unfamiliar instructions. |
| 12 | Take one unopened full Echo mock under exam conditions. Flag every guess. Review the section with the lowest confident accuracy. | Baseline by section, timing and recurring error. |
| 11–8 | First-check untested words and grammar. Each day do a timed passage and a single-play dialogue. Retrieve yesterday’s errors before revealing explanations. | Record the cue that resolved each mistake. Recheck it on another example the following day. |
| 7–6 | Take a fresh full mock on one of these days. Use the other day for targeted repair. | If a section falls below the practice floor, give it the first focused block for the next two days. |
| 5–4 | Spend the largest active block on the weakest section. Give the other sections a short unfamiliar check. Complete the planned first pass if pace allows. | A recurring error in new material takes priority over additional passive playback. |
| 3–2 | Take a different unopened full mock on a separate day. Inspect the sprint checkpoint and repair the weakest remaining distinction. | If results disagree, confidence remains unresolved. Use an unseen passage or dialogue to diagnose the gap. |
| 1 and exam day | Short delayed recall, familiar audio if helpful, logistics and rest. | Avoid drawing a new readiness conclusion from a last-minute retake. |

**Six-hour ceiling.** On a mock day use 155 minutes for the paper, 50 for explanations, 35 for delayed checks, 65 for screening vocabulary and grammar, 15 for radio and 40 for breaks. On a repair day use 35 for delayed checks, 95 for screening, 105 for timed reading and listening (give more to the weaker section), 55 for corrections, 30 for radio and 40 for breaks. Shorten first checks when the work takes longer. These allocations are coaching defaults, not experimentally established dosages.

**What the live checkpoint measures.** Take unopened timed Echo forms seriously, in one sitting with the normal break, without looking up answers. The hub considers each book's first recorded attempt, completed on the starting local calendar day. It inspects the two most recent eligible forms completed within seven calendar days. Each must have at least 60% confident correct in each reporting section and at least a 70% equal-weight average across those three sections. They must fall on different dates. Unanswered and flagged items contribute zero. Personal AI quizzes, retakes and workbook companions are excluded. A later weak first sitting can remove a previously met benchmark. Echo cannot observe outside assistance, prior exposure outside this browser or the comparability of its questions to the real exam.

**Interpretation.** The 70/60 thresholds and seven-day window are conservative design choices without validated predictive accuracy. The official decision uses equated scores. Publish a probability only after consented, linked real exam outcomes support calibration on held-out learners. Until then show the actual evidence, weakest section, and next action. In the final fortnight the standard Today plan requests a baseline when there is no recent qualifying result, a checkpoint in the final week and a last fresh sitting near the exam if evidence is still short. Between sittings it links directly to repair a weak section. New-check rounds yield to the six-hour budget including a 40-minute break allowance. The optional AI plan is a separate recommendation and can differ from this standard path.
